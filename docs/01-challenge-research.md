## 3. Eligibility & Participation Rules

- **Students only** — high school and college level; companies/professional organizations are excluded.

Each team must submit:

1. **Project Title** — clear, memorable.

4. **Project Link / Repository** — GitHub/GitLab/live demo (optional but recommended).

## 7. Sponsor Deep Dive — Who They Are & How to "Support" Them

### 7.1 Adaption Labs (adaptionlabs.ai)
**What they are:** A San Francisco-based frontier AI lab (founded 2025 by Sara Hooker, ex-Cohere/Google Brain, and Sudip Roy, ex-Cohere CTO). Raised $50M seed (Feb 2025) at ~$1B valuation; investors include Emergence Capital, Mozilla Ventures, Fifty Years, Threshold Ventures.

**Core thesis:** "The days of monolithic AI are over." Most AI is static/frozen; Adaption builds **AI that continuously learns and adapts** — "adaptation" as the missing layer between apps and foundation models.

**Three product pillars (their expertise):**
1. **Adaptive Data** — optimize, evolve, and *invent* datasets across **242+ languages**, text, documents, vision. Claimed 82% avg data-quality improvement; 33M+ data artifacts processed. Connects HuggingFace, Kaggle, Google Sheets, direct upload. **"Invent a dataset" — generate new data where none exists, for tasks/domains/languages nobody collected.**
2. **Adaptive Intelligence (AutoScientist)** — set an objective; the system finds the training recipe, runs experiments, and trains a custom model for your task. Co-optimizes data + training recipe; claims 109% relative improvement vs baseline and 49% over human-configured training. Models are **owned by the user** (custom training, serve, evolve). Also offers gradient-free "steerability adapters" and context shaping.
3. **Adaptive Interfaces** — innovation hub for re-imagining human–AI interaction; feedback loops.

**Why they sponsor this challenge:** developer/student adoption, real-world showcase of "adaptive" AI usage, and demonstrating that their credits are useful to builders.

**What "supporting Adaption" means in a project:** the project should **actually use** Adaption (credits, platform, or its concepts — adaptive data, continuous learning, custom model training) and call it out clearly in the description/technologies. Judges and the sponsor read for genuine integration, not name-dropping.

### 7.2 Rise Research (riseglobaleducation.com)
**What they are:** A selective **1-on-1 PhD mentorship program for high school students** where students **publish original research**, win awards, and earn global recognition. Spin-off **Rise Research Data Labs** produces expert post-training data (RL preference data, reasoning traces, evaluations) from a network of 100,000+ academic experts / 10,000+ PhDs from top-20 universities.

**Key stats/claims:** 40+ academic journals published student work; 90% publication success rate; 500+ Ivy League/Oxbridge PhD mentors; 3× higher acceptance to top-10 universities. 10-week 1-on-1 program; Winter 2026 cohort deadline Nov 15, 2026.

**Their expertise/ethos:**
- **Student research as a credential** — original, peer-reviewed, defensible work is the hardest thing to fabricate.
- **AI-as-assistant, not substitute** — AI may support a student's process (grammar, literature search, debugging, question generation) **with disclosure**; using AI to generate the ideas/analysis/prose presented as the student's own is misconduct.
- **Accessibility of research** — AI makes research-level work accessible to students anywhere.

**Why they sponsor this challenge:** aligns with their mission (students doing real AI work), and their Data Labs needs visibility for expert-data pipelines.

**What "supporting Rise Research" means in a project:** build a tool that **serves student research** — helping students find questions, collect/analyze data, structure papers, and *stay honest* (AI assistance with transparency/auditability). The project should embody their ethos: AI assists the student, never replaces the student's thinking; outputs are traceable, disclosed, defensible.

### 7.3 Organizer — ML Empowerment Foundation (mlempowermentfoundation.org)
- Student-led nonprofit, founded 2024. Mission: **"Free AI education, built by students for students"** — free AI curriculum, build challenges, and local chapters so any student, anywhere can learn to build with AI.
- **Reach:** 72,000+ students taught; 2,580+ build-challenge participants; $687,500 in sponsor prizes valued; 14 sponsor partners.
- **Leadership:** Abyan Kashif (President), Anthony Vuong (VP), Chetansai Pothuraju (Treasurer), Shikhar Sisodia (Secretary).
- **Curriculum:** 12-lesson free Udemy course *"AI for Beginners: From Zero to Understanding AI"* (1h25m, no coding required) covering: AI basics, ML/DL, how machines learn, data fundamentals, classic ML algorithms, neural networks, LLMs, multimodal AI, prompt engineering, AI ethics & impact, AI and the future (agentic systems).
- **Their field/expertise:** **structured, beginner-friendly AI education that ends in a shipped real-world AI project**. The winning project should be educational, accessible, and demonstrate AI fundamentals clearly — they explicitly say even a "simple prototype, wireframe, or mockup is sufficient."

**The "their field" triangle that a winning solution must sit in:**
1. **ML Empowerment's field** = AI education / learning-by-building, beginner-accessible, social good.
2. **Adaption's expertise** = adaptive data, continuous learning, custom model training, multilingual/low-resource data invention.
3. **Rise Research's expertise** = high-school student research, peer-reviewed original work, AI-as-assistant-with-disclosure.

## 9. Research Framework: How to Find a Unique, Sponsor-Supporting Idea

**Method (works for this challenge specifically):**

1. **Map the three fields → find the intersection.**
   - ML Empowerment = AI learning + real-world project + student impact.
   - Adaption = adaptive data + continuous learning + custom models + 242 languages.
   - Rise = student research + original work + AI-as-assistant-with-disclosure.
   → **Intersection:** a tool that helps students *do real AI research/learning*, using *adaptive, continuously-learning AI*, built on *data the platform can invent/optimize*, with *transparent, auditable AI assistance*.
2. **Scan the 3.0 gallery (235 projects) and mark anything in the intersection.** (From the sample: essentially nothing covers student-research workflows + adaptive learning. RecallAI and clearmind pro touch learning, but not *research* + *adaptive data* + *integrity*.)
3. **Differentiate on depth, not novelty-of-domain.** Pick a domain already proven to win (education/research access), then add a layer none of the gallery entries has (continuous learning via Adaption, expert-data pipeline via Rise, multilingual data invention).
4. **Verify sponsor alignment by actually using the sponsor product** — Adaption credits are free and given to everyone; using them is expected. Rise alignment is thematic (build for students/research).
5. **Score candidate ideas against the weighted rubric** (30% technical, 20% creativity, 20% impact, 15% UX, 15% presentation) before committing.

## 10. Candidate Solution Directions (Unique, In-Field, Sponsor-Supporting)

*These are direction-level concepts derived from the framework — original to this challenge's field intersection, not copied from the gallery. Pick one and go deep.*

### Direction A' — **"ResearchLoop: Scoped AI Research Helper for High School Students"** *(recommended — A cut down to one vertical slice done well)*
**Revised scope (per buildability review):** A tool where a high-schooler drops in a rough research question (or a tiny messy dataset) and gets **two things, done well**:

1. **Adaption Adaptive Data in action** — the tool *invents or augments* a small dataset for that topic (generate data where none exists, or raise a tiny dataset's quality), producing a demoable **before/after quality chart**. This is a literal, flagship-feature use of Adaption.
2. **A disclosure receipt** — every output shows what the AI did, which sources/data it used, and what remains the student's own contribution. This satisfies Rise's integrity ethos (AI assists, never replaces the student's thinking).

**What was deliberately dropped from the original A:** the full research-cycle workflow (lit search → method → analysis → draft → citation), the continuous-learning/AutoScientist layer, and the multilingual data-invention sub-feature. Those are 4–5 stitched-together systems with high stub risk under time pressure — and judges weight Technical Implementation at 30%, so one feature that visibly works end to end beats five shallow ones.

- **Adaption support:** genuine, literal use of **Adaptive Data** (invent/augment) — the sponsor's flagship capability, shown with a before/after demo.
- **Rise Research support:** the disclosure receipt enforces their ethos (traceable AI assistance, student owns the thinking) — the exact integrity standard they publish about.
- **ML Empowerment support:** beginner-friendly, teaches AI fundamentals (data quality, evidence, ethics), strong social-impact story (democratizing student research access).
- **Technical story for judges:** data generation/augmentation pipeline + LLM-assisted dataset synthesis + receipt/traceability layer + clean UI. Buildable to a working demo by ~day 9, leaving real time for polish and testing (15% presentation weight).
- **Impact story:** students without lab/mentor access can get a real dataset and a defensible, disclosed research starting point.

### Direction B — **"LinguaLab: Inventing Educational AI Datasets in Underrepresented Languages"** *(fallback — strong Adaption demo, thin on impact)*
**What:** A platform where students in low-resource language communities (e.g., Tamil, Bengali, Swahili — Adaption supports 242 languages) *create and quality-check AI datasets* for local learning content, then train small adaptive models for local study tools.

- **Adaption:** literal use of "Invent a dataset" + Adaptive Data quality pipelines; multilingual coverage is the sponsor's flagship capability.
- **Rise:** students doing original data-collection research that can be documented and published; teaches data literacy (the curriculum's "Data: The Foundation of AI" lesson).
- **ML Empowerment:** "any student, anywhere," multilingual AI education, social good.
- **Uniqueness:** no gallery entry tackles low-resource *data creation* + student ownership.
- **Trade-off:** cleaner technical demo than A' but **thinner on Real-World Impact (20%)** — a nice before/after data-quality story rather than a visible human outcome. Choose only if the team prefers the data-engineering path over the research-assistant path.

### Direction C — **"CitedMind: Auditable AI Learning Companion with Evidence Receipts"**
**What:** A study/revision companion where every AI answer carries an evidence receipt — source quotes, confidence, and a claim-verification check (like "Receipts" but generalized to student learning) — combined with a **continuously-adapting personal model** (Adaption) that improves from the student's corrections.

- **Adaption:** continuous learning from corrections; custom model trained per student.
- **Rise:** academic integrity, citation discipline, defensible work.
- **ML Empowerment:** learning tool, ties to the curriculum's ethics + prompt-engineering lessons.
- **Risk:** closest to existing entries (Receipts, RecallAI, clearmind pro) — needs the Adaption continuous-learning layer to stand out.

### Direction D — **"MentorMatch + Research Pipeline: Matching Students to Research with AI"**
**What:** A tool that helps a student discover a research question suited to their interests/skills, drafts a methodology, and produces a submission-ready package — with an **adaptive** recommendation engine (Adaption) and **expert-data-style** quality gates (Rise's Data Labs ethos).

- Strong sponsor alignment; more "platform" in feel, so heavier to build well in 16 days — use only if the team is stronger at data/matching than at UI.

**Selection rule:** choose the direction where your team can *actually build a working prototype* (Technical Implementation = 30%), then polish the presentation (15%). **Direction A' (scoped ResearchLoop)** is the best balance of uniqueness, sponsor support, buildability, and impact — one vertical slice that visibly works end to end. Fall back to B only if the team's strength is data engineering and it accepts a thinner impact story.

## 11. Risk & Constraint Checklist

- **Eligibility:** everyone on the team must be a student (high school/college) above legal age; no companies.
- **No cash prize** — the value is the certificate, Top-10 placement, Adaption credits, and sponsor/organizer visibility. Treat it as a career/portfolio play.
- **Deadline hard:** Dec 2, 2:45 AM EST. Submit early (Dec 1).
- **Do not plagiarize gallery ideas** — build on the *intersection*, not on a specific 3.0 entry.
- **Sponsor use must be genuine:** register via the referral link (free $1,000 credits), actually use Adaption for the adaptive-data/continuous-learning features, and mention it explicitly in "Technologies Used."
- **Rise alignment must respect their ethics:** AI assists, student owns the thinking; include disclosure/traceability — this is both their ethos and a judging positive (ethics is in the curriculum).
- **Beginner-friendly bar:** a working prototype + clear documentation is enough; don't over-engineer.
